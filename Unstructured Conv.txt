## 1. The Form-Factor Question — Answered Directly

**It's hybrid, and here's the actual reasoning, not just a preference:**

Look at who's actually specified in the problem statement: **All India Institute of Ayurveda (AIIA)** — a national apex tertiary institute, not a remote PHC hut. That matters. It already has registration desks, some computers, OPD counters, decent power/connectivity. So the "no infrastructure exists" assumption is wrong for the primary deployment target.

But the patient population described (elderly, rural, low-literacy, first-visit, "excluding smartphone-less populations") means you can't assume everyone carries a phone either.

So the honest answer is **one software system, three access points**, not three different products:

| Access point | Who it's for | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| **Kiosk mode** | Walk-in patients with no smartphone | Your same web app, running fullscreen/locked ("kiosk mode") on the hospital's *existing* tablets/desktops at OPD counters — this is a browser flag, not new hardware. This is your "ATM/airport check-in" feel, achieved in software. |
| **Personal device / home PWA** | Smartphone owners | Same app opened on their own phone — can even start history-taking *before* they leave home, or in the waiting hall on their own device |
| **Assisted mode** | Zero digital literacy, illiterate, very elderly | A volunteer/ASHA/registration clerk operates the identical app on their own device, voice-driving it on the patient's behalf |

One backend, one data model, three doors in. This is *why* it's software track and not hardware track — the "kiosk" is a UI mode, not a machine you build.

## 2. What Actually Differentiates This for SIH 2026

Quick fact check since this affects your strategy: **SIH 2026 launched Aug 21, 2026** — you're currently in the internal-hackathon → idea/PPT+video national-screening phase (most colleges' PPT deadlines fall in early-mid September), with the 36-hour Grand Finale in December. So right now, **the thing being judged is the idea+PPT+video, not a finished product.** That changes what "differentiation" means.

The Smart India Hackathon 2026 launched on August 21, 2026, and SIH 2026 covers 226 released problem statements (172 software + 54 hardware) — healthcare AI is a crowded category. Most teams touching a "patient intake" problem will submit a generic symptom-checker chatbot. Here's precisely where they'll fail against *this specific* problem statement, and where you should double down:

- **They'll skip AYUSH entirely.** A generic allopathic triage bot doesn't answer a Ministry of Ayush brief. Dashavidha Pariksha is named explicitly in the statement — if your demo can't show mode-switching to it, you've missed the actual ask.
- **They'll assume smartphone ownership.** The statement explicitly calls out that apps "exclude the elderly, rural, low-literacy... population who form the bulk of OPD load." A slick app-only pitch fails this line directly.
- **They'll treat documents as an afterthought.** Most will build the conversational bot and bolt on "OCR support" as a footnote. The fragmented-paper-records problem is given equal weight in the statement — build it as a first-class pipeline, not a checkbox.
- **They'll forget the doctor is the actual user of the output.** The whole point is cutting the 2-5 minute bottleneck. If your summary is a wall of AI-generated text the doctor still has to read fully, you haven't solved the stated problem — the output needs to be skimmable in seconds.
- **Nobody else will tie Ayurvedic and allopathic medicine together safety-wise.** The herb-drug interaction check is the one feature that's *only* relevant because this is an AYUSH ministry brief — it's your strongest "why us" line in the pitch.

Practical advice: in your PPT, map every feature to a literal sentence from the problem statement. Judges screening 200+ decks reward obvious traceability over cleverness they have to infer.

## 3. UI / Screen Flow

Roughly in order a patient moves through:

1. **Welcome & language select** — large regional-script buttons + flags/icons, spoken welcome in each language on tap (zero-reading required to get started)
2. **Identify** — ABHA QR scan / Aadhaar-linked mobile OTP / "I'm a new patient" path; assisted-mode toggle visible here too
3. **Consent** — audio-narrated, granular toggles (voice recording, document scan, data sharing), a "hear this again" button, big clear yes/no
4. **Mode auto-select** — Allopathic vs Ayurvedic history flow, ideally pre-set by which OPD counter/department the kiosk sits at
5. **Conversational history** — mic button + equivalent tap options shown together always, live captions of what it heard, a progress bar (Complaint → HPI → Past history → Drugs/Allergy → Family → ROS), and an edit/back option at each step
6. **AYUSH picture-based module** (when active) — swipeable icon cards for digestion, energy, sleep, appetite, body frame — tap the picture that matches, no text required
7. **Document scan** — camera guide overlay to align paper, batch capture, live preview of what OCR extracted so the patient/volunteer can confirm or correct before it's finalized
8. **Scheme eligibility card** — auto-surfaced based on details already given, with a "send me the checklist" button (SMS/WhatsApp, not app-locked)
9. **Red-flag interstitial** — full-screen override that interrupts everything else and sends the patient straight to a nurse if danger symptoms are detected
10. **Confirm & submit** — the system speaks the summary back in plain language ("You told me...") so the patient can catch anything wrong before it's locked
11. **Token & wait screen** — token number, department, and either a simple text direction or a floor icon — no live indoor GPS needed
12. **Physician screen (separate view)** — structured, section-by-section summary that's accept/edit/reject per line, the document timeline, red-flag banner if any, and the herb-drug warning if flagged — never an autonomous diagnosis, always a draft the doctor owns

## 4. The End-to-End Pipeline (Conceptually)

1. **Entry** — patient reaches the system through any of the 3 access points; identity + consent captured
2. **Conversational capture** — speech goes to ASR (Bhashini/IndicWhisper) in short turns → transcript → an LLM dialogue manager constrained to a clinical ontology (SOCRATES for allopathic, Dashavidha Pariksha for AYUSH) decides the next question or marks the section complete
3. **Document capture (parallel)** — camera images → cleanup (deskew, contrast) → OCR → an extraction LLM pulls diagnoses, drugs, dosages, lab values into structured fields, ordered by date
4. **Continuous safety check** — a red-flag classifier watches the conversation live; once medications are known (from either voice or OCR), the herb-drug interaction check runs against the combined list
5. **Synthesis** — a summarization step merges the conversational history and the digitized documents into one physician-format note
6. **Eligibility check** — a separate rules engine runs against the demographic fields already captured, independent of the medical pipeline
7. **Consent-gated push** — the structured note and document timeline are sent to the hospital's system and, in the sandbox/certified flow, linked to ABHA
8. **Routing** — a token and department are assigned based on the chief complaint/triage outcome
9. **Consult** — the doctor sees the structured draft the moment the patient walks in, edits or confirms it, and that becomes the actual medical record
10. **Teardown** — temporary local session data is wiped once submission completes, per the consent design

## 5. 12+ More Feature Ideas (New Ones, Not Repeats)

1. **Visit-to-visit memory** — for returning ABHA-linked patients, the system opens with "last time you reported X — how's that now?" instead of restarting from zero. Directly attacks the "repeated questioning across visits" line in the problem statement.
2. **Caregiver/proxy mode** — for infants, unconscious, or severely elderly patients, a parent/attendant answers, and the record clearly tags it as *caregiver-reported* vs *patient-reported* — clinically meaningful distinction most teams will miss.
3. **Closed-loop discharge translator** — after the consult, the doctor's prescription/instructions get converted into simple audio + icon-based home-care guidance (sun/moon icons for medicine timing) in the patient's own language. Most solutions stop at intake; this closes the other end.
4. **SMS/IVR follow-up and adherence reminders** — no app required, works on basic feature phones, reaches patients without smartphones for medicine timing or next-visit reminders.
5. **"How long until my turn" live text updates** — patients leave the crowded physical waiting hall and get a text when they're a few patients away — a genuine operational win for OPD overcrowding, not just a data feature.
6. **Visual symptom-timeline builder** — instead of asking "since when," patients tap "today / few days / weeks / months" on a simple visual scale — much easier than a date picker for low-literacy users, still gives the doctor a usable HPI.
7. **Low-confidence triage flag** — if the AI isn't confident which department fits the symptoms, it routes to general medicine screening first instead of guessing a specialist — reduces misrouting in a large referral hospital.
8. **ASHA/sub-center record continuity** — if a patient was already screened at a village health worker level before referral, that record (where digitized) pre-fills the session instead of restarting — continuity across India's actual referral chain.
9. **Family batch sessions** — one consent/identify flow can branch into multiple linked patient profiles, since patients frequently arrive as a family unit rather than one at a time.
10. **Reverse-direction doctor dictation** — after the consult, the doctor can voice-dictate exam findings/diagnosis back into the same system, appending to the record. This gives doctors a reason to actually adopt it (a voice shortcut, not another typing burden).
11. **Anonymized OPD analytics for hospital administration** — aggregated, de-identified trends (common complaints by day, department load, seasonal surges) as a genuine "Smart Automation" angle aimed at hospital management, not just patients.
12. **"Why am I being asked this?" explainer** — a small audio/text button next to unusual AYUSH questions (bowel habits, sleep, diet) so patients don't feel confused or suspicious — improves completion rates and trust.
13. **Rough cost estimator** — paired with the scheme checker, once a likely department/test path is known, show an approximate cost range with vs. without scheme coverage — addresses the real financial anxiety driving healthcare decisions in India.
14. **Toll-free IVR fallback** — for patients who can't or won't approach even a touchscreen, the same conversational intake runs over a phone call (pure voice, no screen at all) — extends reach past even the kiosk/assisted modes.
15. **Printed QR receipt** — a physical paper slip with a QR code after the session, for patients uncomfortable relying purely on a digital record — gives them something tangible to hold, which matters culturally in a paper-trust context.

For your PPT specifically: pick maybe 3-4 of these (the visit-memory, closed-loop discharge, and IVR fallback are your strongest differentiators) rather than cramming all 15 — judges scoring 200+ decks reward depth on a few over breadth on everything, and it also keeps the December 36-hour build scope realistic.

## ML + Offline: Is Dual Architecture Doable?

Short answer: **yes, but not uniformly** — and the honest breakdown matters more than a blanket "yes":

**Doesn't need ML at all, works offline trivially:**
- The AYUSH touch/icon-based Dashavidha Pariksha module — it's just structured UI logic (tap the picture that matches), no model required either way
- The scheme-eligibility checker — should be a deterministic rules engine, not an LLM call, regardless of connectivity (more auditable, and judges will trust a rules engine over "the AI decided you're eligible")
- Basic red-flag safety net — the emergency-symptom trigger is exactly the kind of thing that should **not** depend on a model or connectivity. A lightweight keyword/rule-based layer that always runs locally, with the LLM as an *additional* layer only when online, is the safer design here — a safety-critical check shouldn't have a single point of failure that's "no internet."

**Needs ML, degrades gracefully offline:**
- OCR — Tesseract/PaddleOCR run fully on-device already, no cloud dependency; only the LLM-based *entity extraction* on top (turning raw OCR text into structured drug names/dosages) needs a model, and a small quantized one can run locally at reduced accuracy
- Voice ASR — a distilled/quantized local model (Whisper-tiny/base class, or a compressed IndicWhisper) can run offline, but accuracy will be visibly worse than the full cloud-hosted model. That's a real tradeoff, not a free win

**Genuinely hard to fully replicate offline:**
- The adaptive dialogue manager (deciding the *next best question* based on everything said so far) — a small local LLM can approximate this, but quality drops noticeably against a large cloud model. This is where "dual architecture" earns its name: a lightweight local model for offline sessions, swapped for the full model when connectivity returns.

**On implementation** — you've actually already solved this exact problem once. Sahakar's `model_router.rs` / `brain_switcher.rs` is precisely a "route between local and larger models based on availability/constraints" pattern. That's directly reusable thinking here: MediKiosk's offline/online switch is the same architectural shape — router checks connectivity, picks the local quantized model or the cloud one, everything downstream (dialogue manager, extraction, summarizer) doesn't need to know which one answered.

**Practical scope for a 36-hour build:** don't try to make everything work offline. Pick the "store-and-forward" pattern as your baseline (capture audio/images/answers locally via IndexedDB, sync when signal returns) and layer the local-model fallback on top only for the adaptive-questioning piece as your stretch/demo-differentiator — that's a strong, honest "we thought about rural connectivity" story without overclaiming full offline parity.
